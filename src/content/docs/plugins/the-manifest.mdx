A plugin is one `plugin.toml` at the root of its repository or directory. A
source declaring two plugins is refused.

The reference below is not written here. It is read out of
`contract/plugin-manifest.schema.json` at the revision this site pins — the
schema the binary is generated from and validates against, and the one
`lemonfiber plugin schema` prints for your editor. It starts at the root,
`PluginManifest`, and every type a field names links to its own entry. The
normative prose, with the reasons, is
[the plugin manifest contract](/spec/20-architecture/contracts/plugin-manifest/).

## What the reader refuses that a schema cannot say

A manifest can match the schema and still be refused: an `id` with a character
that is not a lower-case letter, a digit or a hyphen, a `port` with no `bind`, a
`hostname` on a service nobody proxies, a `[requires]` naming something this
build does not offer. The rules, each with its reason, are the contract's
[validation](/spec/20-architecture/contracts/plugin-manifest/#validation), and
the fields a manifest has no room for at all — environment, kernel
capabilities, devices, privilege, a fragment of Compose — are
[the fields that are absent, and why](/spec/20-architecture/contracts/plugin-manifest/#the-fields-that-are-absent-and-why).
Neither is restated here, because the specification says the schema is the only
description of the format published to authors
([`F10-R2`](/spec/10-functional/features/f-extensibility/f10-authoring/)), and
a second list is one more to fall behind.

The way to know what this build refuses is to ask it.
[`lemonfiber plugin claims <path>`](/commands/reference/plugin/) reads your
manifest the way `install` will, and reports every violation in one pass rather
than stopping at the first. A refusal is total: nothing of a refused manifest is
acted on, and [`PLUGIN-3`](/fixing/every-error-by-code/#plugin--installing-and-running-plugins)
lists everything that was wrong with it.

Most of what a plugin declares is about its own service. An **extension point**
is different: a published place where a plugin may add a row to lemonfiber
itself. At the revision this site pins there are two extension points, and both
belong to the doctor — a check it runs, and the remedy it shows when that check
does not pass. Each one below names what a manifest has to declare in
`[requires]` to use it.

## The rules every row keeps

- **Namespaced.** A contributed id is `<plugin-id>:<name>`, and may not take an
  id a bundled row holds, by exact name or inside a family the bundled rows own.
- **A check has a remedy.** Every contributed check needs at least one, and a
  remedy's `for` names a check of the same plugin.
- **Rendered, never run.** A remedy is text the error renderer shows you. A
  repair that acts would be a recipe, and recipes are not run by this build.
- **Its own service only.** A contributed check asks the plugin's own service
  and nothing else.
- **Beside, not instead.** A contributed check runs alongside the bundled ones
  and cannot replace, reorder or silence one.

A contributed check runs with every `lemonfiber doctor`, and on its own with
`lemonfiber doctor --only <plugin-id>:<name>`. One that does not hold is raised
as
[`PLUGIN-1`](/fixing/every-error-by-code/#plugin--installing-and-running-plugins),
carrying the plugin's own remedy. There is no dashboard panel, command or
acting repair a plugin can add; those places are fixed.

The contract, with its reasons, is
[extension points](/spec/20-architecture/contracts/extension-points/).

## Every point

which is also what `lemonfiber plugin extension-points` prints. The fields a row
